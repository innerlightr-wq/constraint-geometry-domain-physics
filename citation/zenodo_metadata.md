## Description

A conceptual synthesis note on the division of labor between binary-partition constraint geometry and domain physics.

The constraint layer supplies the conserved partition $a+b=1$, the Thales altitude $h=\sqrt{ab}$, the asymmetry $\chi=a-b$, the response $R=1/(1-\chi^{2})=1/(4h^{2})$, the rapidity $\xi=\operatorname{arctanh}\chi$, the exact identities among these coordinates, and a short list of intrinsic relational landmarks — principally the altitude–ratio crossing $h=a/b$, equivalent on the partition to $a=b^{3}$.

The domain layer supplies the identity of the two sectors, the reason they exhaust a fixed total, the map $a/b=F(X)$ between a physical variable and the partition coordinates, the trajectory through the manifold, the admissible physical range, and the empirical meaning of any landmark.

Neither layer produces a physical diagnostic alone. A diagnostic emerges only where the two intersect under an independently justified map, and the note shows that an arbitrary choice of $F$ can place a landmark at any desired value — so the burden of proof resides entirely in the embedding.

The primary example is the perturbative decomposition of the Einstein tensor into linear and higher-order parts, for which general relativity motivates the schematic sector scaling $a/b\sim C$ with $C$ the compactness, and the intrinsic crossing then reads $C(1+C)^{2}=1$. A flat-ΛCDM illustration separates two failure modes on one trajectory: a landmark derived from the domain dynamics but unoccupied, and a landmark occupied but provably mechanism-free. The note also gives a five-step protocol, nine safeguards, and an explicit three-tier epistemic classification.

The note introduces no new theorem, no new physical law, and no empirical validation. It consolidates and clarifies material developed across earlier work in the same programme.
