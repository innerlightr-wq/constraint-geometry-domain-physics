# Example: Newtonian Two-Body Gravity

The cleanest **empty** case, and for that reason the most instructive of the minor examples: an exact embedding with a physically meaningless landmark.

## A genuine partition

Fixed total mass gives a partition for a domain-internal reason. With $c=m_{1}+m_{2}=M$ and hypotenuse segments $m_{1},m_{2}$, the Euclidean altitude theorem gives

$$m_{1}+m_{2}=M,\qquad h^{2}=m_{1}m_{2}$$

exactly. So the mass product appearing in Newton's force law *is* the squared Thales altitude of a right triangle on a diameter $M$.

## What the geometry organizes

The gravitational force and potential energy are altitude-squared quantities:

$$F_{\mathrm{grav}}=\frac{Gh^{2}}{r^{2}},\qquad U=-\frac{Gh^{2}}{r}$$

and the standard derived masses acquire geometric readings:

$$\mu=\frac{m_{1}m_{2}}{M}=\frac{h^{2}}{c},\qquad \nu=\frac{m_{1}m_{2}}{M^{2}}=\frac{h^{2}}{c^{2}}$$

so the reduced mass is the altitude squared per unit hypotenuse, and the symmetric mass ratio is the squared altitude-to-hypotenuse ratio. The coupling efficiency $\eta=4h^{2}/c^{2}=4\nu$ reaches unity at the apex $m_{1}=m_{2}$.

There is real organizational content here — the **coupling–timing asymmetry**. Force and potential energy are altitude-governed, while orbital velocity and Kepler timing depend on the hypotenuse $c=M$ and not on $h$ at all. The chirp mass sits at a fractional order between them, $\mathcal{M}=h^{6/5}/c^{1/5}$. All of these are exact re-expressions of standard relations, not new results.

## The landmark, and its emptiness

In normalized form $a=m_{1}/M$, $b=m_{2}/M$, the embedding is the mass ratio itself:

$$F(q)=q,\qquad q=\frac{m_{1}}{m_{2}}$$

This is as good as an embedding gets — **exact, not schematic, with no undetermined normalization.** The intrinsic marker is therefore realized at the single mass ratio

$$q_{*}=r_{*}\approx0.4656,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \frac{m_{2}}{m_{1}}=\frac{1}{r_{*}}=\psi^{2}\approx2.1479$$

**And nothing happens there.** Newtonian mechanics supplies the physical laws and the observables, and it assigns no distinguished role to that mass ratio. No orbit changes character, no stability boundary is crossed, no observable acquires a feature. The intrinsic marker exists in the partition, is exactly located, and is physically empty in this domain.

## Why this case matters to the argument

It isolates a failure mode the other examples cannot. The perturbative-GR case is weak because its embedding is schematic; one might suppose that a better embedding would rescue the landmark. The two-body case refutes that supposition directly: here the embedding is **perfect** and the landmark is still empty.

So a derived embedding is necessary and not sufficient. The domain must also assign the landmark a role, and no amount of geometric exactness can supply one.

This is the third of the three distinguishable negative results:

| Failure | Where it occurs | Diagnosis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Partition not genuine | Step 1 | nothing downstream can be repaired |
| Embedding chosen, not derived | Step 5 | any numerical agreement is manufactured |
| Landmark real, embedding derived, domain silent | Step 4 | **transit marker** — correct, exactly located, physically empty |

The two-body case is the cleanest instance of the third, and flat ΛCDM's cubic state is a second.
